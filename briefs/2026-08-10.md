## F2.4 — ARCHITECT: the oracle, written before the artifact existed

`oracles/011-diffusion-curves/CONTRACT.md` was committed with **no artifact in the tree**, and it
names the four separable ways this build could be false: it isn't running in the graphics pipeline ·
it doesn't honour the boundary data · **the field it produces is not harmonic** (a blur, or an
inverse-distance blend, looks almost identical to the eye and is a different function) · it isn't
really a Monte Carlo estimator. `oracle.py` drives a real Chromium against those, 25 predicates.

The predicate that carries the ship is **P3, the mean-value property**: a harmonic function's value
at a point equals its average over any circle around that point clear of the boundary. That is the
*definition*, it is checkable on the rendered pixels alone, and no blur satisfies it. Measured on the
build: **mean 1.72, max 5.72** (tolerance 6.0 / 22.0, derived from the Monte-Carlo noise model in
CONTRACT §3 before the build existed — the model predicted ~2.0).

**PROBE-THE-ORACLE — 10 negative controls, 4 stated break attempts, and the part that matters is
what it found in the oracle rather than in the page.** The headline break attempt was constructed
before the controls were written: *an affine ramp is harmonic*, so a plain linear gradient satisfies
P3 exactly. Control C2 runs that attack — P3 passes it, P2 catches it — which is why the harmonic
test scene carries three curves whose two-sided colour data no affine function can match, and why P2
is a separate predicate instead of folded into P3.

**Three predicates turned out to be decoration, and their own controls are how that surfaced.**

- **P7** accepted the page's own startup auto-render as proof the Render button worked. A completely
